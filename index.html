A useful starting point

A simple lead follow-up workflow

A lightweight process can work in a notebook or spreadsheet. The key is that every open conversation has an owner, a next step, and a date to revisit it.

1

Capture the essentials

Record the person or business, how they reached you, what they need, and when you last spoke. Keep only information you need to do the work.

2

Write the next action

Use a specific task such as “send the scope” or “ask whether Tuesday works” instead of a vague note like “follow up.”

3

Close the loop

After each conversation, update the status, set the next date, or mark the opportunity closed. Review open items on a regular schedule that suits your workload.

Keep client data privateUse a secure tool appropriate for the information you handle. Avoid putting confidential client details into a public page or an unapproved service.
Tool selection

When is a CRM worth adding?

A customer-management tool is useful when the current process makes it easy to miss a promise or lose track of a conversation. Compare tools against your own workflow instead of choosing by feature count.

QuestionWhat to checkWhy it matters
Can I see the next action?Tasks, reminders, and a clear view of open conversationsPrevents follow-up from living only in memory
Will it fit my process?Contact fields, stages, and a way to export your dataReduces setup work and makes it easier to switch later
What will it cost later?Current plan limits, paid features, seats, and billing termsA free starting plan may not include every feature you need
Can I use it responsibly?Access controls, privacy information, and data handling termsClient information deserves careful handling

Protect your time

Should you do the task yourself or hire help?

Outsourcing can make sense when a task is repeatable, clearly defined, and taking time away from paid work. First make the outcome and access requirements explicit.

Write a clear brief

  • Describe the result you need and what “done” means.
  • List the tools and information the worker will need.
  • Use the least access needed; remove access when the work ends.
  • Agree on milestones, review points, and a spending limit.

Keep sensitive work under review

  • Do not share payment credentials or account passwords.
  • Review work before it affects clients or public materials.
  • Check ownership and confidentiality terms before sharing files.
  • Compare the total cost with the time you expect to save.
